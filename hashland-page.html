The design question / PDF p. 1

How could participation
go beyond paying to win?

My concept study started with a concern about pay-to-win systems and unequal barriers to participation. I explored a game economy where players could own collectible assets and receive rewards through play, with optional asset purchases.

That was the design ambition. The work connected three questions: what gives a collectible its value, how it becomes useful in a game, and how progression interacts with the economy.

01 / Concept designOriginal portfolio · p. 1

Mapping the
asset system.

I mapped collection value, intellectual property, and real-world hashrate collateral into a shared asset-synthesis model. The diagram then connects synthesis and tokenization to NFT ownership and collectible game characters.

The same page considers three protocol layers: binding hashrate to NFTs, admitting hashrate providers, and creating new composite NFT assets.

The design task was to connect the asset’s economic structure with a character that players could collect and use.

02 / Comparative analysis & tokenomicsOriginal portfolio · p. 2

Comparing the systems
behind the games.

My study compared Axie, MOBOX, and HashLand across entry barriers, gameplay modes, NFT circulation, and reward structures. It examined how the economic model shaped participation alongside the game itself.

  • ParticipationHow difficult is it for a player to enter?
  • GameplayWhich solo, competitive, and group activities are supported?
  • Asset circulationHow do collection, trading, and upgrades fit together?
  • RewardsWhere are incentives directed across the ecosystem?

The adjacent tokenomics study maps HC allocation and application scenarios, including in-game use, NFT trading, and staking.

03 / Gameplay designOriginal portfolio · p. 3

Connecting play
with progression.

The gameplay study connects collectible characters with a 3D ARPG and four activity types. Progression rewards and seasonal rankings give the economic system different participation patterns.

My game design and GameFi mechanism design work considered these activities together with card tiers, upgrade consumption, and reward allocation.

PvE
Individual progression through levels, with completion rewards.
PvP
Competitive matches, with seasonal rewards based on individual points.
GvE
Three-player boss encounters and team completion rewards.
GvG
Guild competition, with seasonal rewards based on guild rankings.

05 / ReflectionLooking back · PDF p. 4

What needed
to improve.

My original portfolio records two limitations after the launch: the game needed more content, and the economic system could become unpredictable and unstable. Strong NFT sales did not resolve those design problems.

User feedback from HashLand informed later concept work on Sealem Lab: a broader platform that could support third-party games, with mechanisms intended to make the economy more stable.

The lasting lesson was to evaluate gameplay depth and economic behavior alongside launch traction.
